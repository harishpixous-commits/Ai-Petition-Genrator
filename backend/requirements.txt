# Document reading for attachments. A citizen brings a photograph of an
# acknowledgement slip, a scanned petition, a ward's complaint spreadsheet, or
# a .doc from a department that has not changed template since 2003. Measured
# against this service's own readers on files produced by Office itself,
# xberg read twelve formats they could not read at all — scans and
# photographs through bundled OCR, the legacy .doc/.ppt/.xls binaries, and
# spreadsheets with their tables intact — matched them on five, and lost on
# one malformed .pptx, which is why the built-in readers remain as a fallback.
#
# MIT. No Python dependencies of its own. No network and no model download at
# runtime, which is what makes it usable in a department deployment with
# neither. Prebuilt wheels cover this image (manylinux_2_28, and bookworm is
# glibc 2.36).
#
# OPTIONAL BY DESIGN: `app/services/document_intelligence.py` falls back to
# the built-in readers when the import fails, so a platform without a wheel
# degrades to the previous behaviour instead of failing to start.
xberg>=1.2,<2
